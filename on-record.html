On Record, Q2 2026

What 28 million streams taught us about releasing music.

We tracked 34 new releases across Sahaya for three months. Some took off. Some disappeared. Some only started moving weeks after release.

Here are the five things we’d want to know before spending money on our next record.

01

A campaign rents attention. The algorithm decides what you keep.

Both campaigns we tracked lifted streams while the money was being spent. The paid gains didn’t last on their own.

But one record set off algorithmic discovery afterwards, reaching around 1,400 unpaid streams a day at its peak.

Don’t just ask whether a campaign got you streams. Ask what was left after it ended.

03

A slow first week doesn’t mean you’ve lost.

One of the slowest releases we tracked had five streams in its first week. A month later it was doing 7,500 a week. Meanwhile the quarter’s biggest launch had lost two-thirds of its pace by week four.

Don’t write a record off because release week was quiet.

04

Stop obsessing over Discover Weekly.

Everyone talks about Discover Weekly. Our data says Radio and Mixes did far more of the work.

If Radio and Mixes start picking your record up, that’s the signal to watch.

05

Your daily numbers are mostly noise.

A bad Tuesday doesn’t mean the record is dying. A great Wednesday doesn’t mean it’s breaking.

Across thousands of predictions, the direction of a record over a week told us far more than any single day.

So what should you actually do?

Four things to take into your next release.

  • Don’t panic in week one.

    Give the record time to show you what it’s doing.

  • Watch what happens after exposure.

    Saves and repeat listening matter more than a temporary spike.

  • Look beyond Discover Weekly.

    Radio and Mixes can be much bigger signals.

  • Read weeks, not days.

    One good or bad day doesn’t tell you where a record is going.
