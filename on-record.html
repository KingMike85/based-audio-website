Five things we learned

What separated the releases that kept climbing from the ones that disappeared. Some of it will change how you spend your next release budget.

Finding 1 of 5

A campaign rents attention. The algorithm decides what you keep.

Two paid campaigns ran on Sahaya artists this quarter: one behind an established pop release, one behind a three-track catalogue starting from almost nothing. Both worked while the money was on. Campaign 1 took its release from roughly 2,000 to 2,900 streams a day for a month. Campaign 2 took a near-dormant catalogue to a peak in the low thousands inside two weeks.

Neither set of paid streams held up on its own. Within ten days of spend ending, Campaign 1 had dropped to around a quarter of its pre-campaign pace. Campaign 2 had fallen 91% from its peak before its spend had even finished.

Then the part that matters. Within days of Campaign 2's spend finishing, just after the quarter closed, Discover Weekly picked up the catalogue, unpaid, reaching around 1,400 algorithmic streams a day at its peak. The paid streams were rented. What the campaign planted in the algorithm wasn't.

Finding 2 of 5

Saves keep the door open.

Two releases sat at opposite ends. Release D peaked above 2,000 streams a day, but fewer than one in a hundred listeners saved it. Within weeks it was in single digits.

Release J never had a big spike and never needed one. One in five listeners saved it, and its base held steady all quarter at a level Spotify kept feeding, with no campaign behind it at all. Exposure opens the door. Saves keep it open.

Finding 3 of 5

Your first week doesn't decide anything.

We compared the first 30 days of every Q2 release with at least four weeks of data. Release B had by far the biggest launch of the quarter and had lost two-thirds of that pace by week 4.

Release C logged five streams in its entire first week. A month later it was doing 7,500 a week. Across all eight releases, week-one volume did not predict which ones would still be growing a month on.

One caution: a few week-four totals were inflated by single-day spikes rather than steady growth. That's why we judge a release on its trajectory and engagement, never on one week's total. Every surge we saw in Q2 decayed. What separated the winners was the floor left behind, and that floor tracked saves and repeat listens, not launch size.

Finding 4 of 5

Stop chasing Discover Weekly.

From May, when we started tracking where algorithmic plays come from, 31.5% of all streams were algorithmic: 7.0M in total. Radio and algorithmic Mixes did the heavy lifting. Discover Weekly, the one everybody talks about, barely registered.

Editorial playlists added 2.78M streams across the quarter. Real, but algorithmic surfaces out-delivered them roughly 2.5 to 1. And the biggest gains came after Spotify gained confidence in a track, not on release day. The path we kept seeing: release, campaign exposure, listener signals build up, Radio and Mixes pick it up, sustained growth.

Finding 5 of 5

Your daily numbers are mostly noise.

Sahaya logs a prediction for every artist, every day, then grades it against what really happened. 4,833 were graded in Q2.

Weekly direction is readable: our model called whether an artist was rising, flat or falling 49.2% of the time, against 40.8% for simply assuming last week would repeat. Next-day calls are a different story. Even our model only hit 15.1%, and plain "same as yesterday" beat it.

We publish that number on purpose. If a model watching your data every day can't call tomorrow, you shouldn't be making decisions off one day's rise or dip either.

Three things to stop believing

You'll hear all of these from other artists, from managers, from people selling you playlists. The quarter's data says otherwise.

  • The biggest first day wins

    The quarter's biggest launch dropped to a third of its launch pace by week 4. A release with five first-week streams was doing 7,500 a week a month later.

  • Discover Weekly is the goal

    It delivered 64,750 streams across every artist on Sahaya in Q2. That's under 1% of the 7.0M algorithmic streams we saw. Radio and Mixes are where the volume is.

  • Your daily numbers tell you how you're doing

    Weekly direction can be read. Exact next-day calls did worse than assuming nothing changes. Reading meaning into one day is reading noise.

The takeaway

Spend buys exposure. Listeners decide if Spotify keeps going.

What to do with this

Every paid campaign we measured lifted streams while the money was on. In every case, the paid streams themselves faded. What mattered was the handover: one campaign gave its gains back, the other seeded an unpaid Discover Weekly pickup.

  • Don't judge a release on week one.

    And don't kill a campaign on week-one numbers either. One of the quarter's slowest starters was doing 7,500 streams a week a month later.

  • Watch for Radio and Mixes.

    Pick-up there was the most reliable early sign of sustained growth we saw all quarter.

  • Read your week, not your day.

    Weekly trend direction is learnable. Daily moves are mostly noise.

  • Treat paid exposure as an audition.

    Budget for it, but know that saves and repeat listens decide what survives once the spend stops.
